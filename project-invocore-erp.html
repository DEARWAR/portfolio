The problem

Why this needed to exist

Small businesses often run on a mix of spreadsheets, paper records, invoice files and separate payment trackers. Customer and vendor details sit in different files, stock quantities drift away from real sales and purchases, partial payments are hard to follow, and monthly summaries have to be stitched together by hand.

The solution

What I built

InvoCore ERP connects the core operational records. Finalising a sales invoice reduces stock; accepting a purchase order increases it. Payments are recorded against invoices and vendor purchases, and the dashboard, ledgers and exports are built from the same records.

Key features

What it can do

Customers & vendors

One party directory with tax / GST details, bank details and customer / vendor / both classification.

Products & inventory

SKU, HSN, categories, units, prices, GST rates, opening stock and a stock ledger of every movement.

Sales invoicing

Items, quantities, discounts and tax with draft / final states and downloadable PDFs using company branding.

Purchase orders

Draft and accepted orders with currency, delivery dates, charges and PDF output; accepting increases stock.

Payments & ledgers

Customer payments with Paid / Partial / Pending status and a party-wise accounts control panel.

Dashboard & reports

Monthly sales vs accepted purchases, six-month summary, Excel and CSV exports.

Company profile

Company name, GSTIN, bank details and logo reused in every generated document.

Access control

Create users and choose which modules each can see.

Honest scope

What is built — and what is not

I like being clear about where a project actually stands, so you know exactly what you are looking at.

✅ Built & working

  • Customer and vendor management
  • Product catalogue with stock ledger and opening stock
  • Sales invoices with draft / final flow and PDF generation
  • Purchase orders with accept → stock increase
  • Customer payment tracking and party-wise ledger
  • Vendor payments and purchase ledger
  • Dashboard, six-month summary, Excel / CSV exports
  • Company profile and document branding
  • User creation with module access

🚧 Not built yet

  • Full accounting / tax-filing — dashboard figure is sales minus accepted purchases, not a true profit-and-loss
  • Real email sending (email helpers currently only log)
  • Complete server-side permission enforcement — access flags mainly control sidebar modules
  • Production hardening of authentication, secrets, validation and deployment

Reflection

What I learned

  • Tying stock movement to document state (final / accepted) keeps inventory honest.
  • Be explicit about what a dashboard number is — sales minus purchases is not profit.
  • Permissions must be enforced on the server, not just hidden in the UI.
